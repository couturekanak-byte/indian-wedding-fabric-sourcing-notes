# Fabric Sourcing Guide | Textile Knowledge & Designer Fabric Selection

**A practical resource for fashion designers, boutique owners, textile enthusiasts, and independent clothing brands.**

Choosing the right fabric is the foundation of a well-designed garment. Whether you're developing a wedding collection, creating everyday clothing, or sourcing materials for a fashion business, understanding fabric properties helps you make better decisions.

This repository explores popular textile categories, fabric quality checks, garment suitability, and sourcing considerations for designers and wholesale buyers.

## How to Choose the Right Fabric

### 1. Consider the Garment Silhouette

Different fabrics create different shapes.

- **Flowing outfits:** Georgette and suitable crepe fabrics.
- **Structured garments:** Brocade and crisp organza.
- **Embellished outfits:** Embroidered net and decorative silk fabrics.
- **Comfort-focused clothing:** Cotton and suitable lightweight blends.

Always evaluate the material's weight, transparency, flexibility, and drape before finalizing a design.

### 2. Check Fabric Quality

Before purchasing fabric, inspect:

- Fibre composition and fabric width.
- Colour consistency under different lighting.
- Embroidery, sequins, and beadwork.
- Surface texture and fabric strength.
- Shrinkage and care requirements.
- Suitability for lining and stitching.

For wholesale purchases, request a swatch whenever possible.

### 3. Calculate Fabric Requirements

Fabric meterage depends on the garment design, body measurements, fabric width, and cutting layout.

A flared lehenga generally requires a different quantity of material from a straight-cut kurta.

Consult your tailor or pattern maker before placing an order.

## Common Fabric Buying Mistakes

**Choosing fabric based only on photographs:** Images may not accurately represent texture, colour, or drape.

**Ignoring fabric width:** Incorrect width assumptions can increase material requirements.

**Skipping quality checks:** Loose embellishments, uneven embroidery, or unsuitable finishing can create stitching problems.

**Buying excessive inventory:** Small brands should test demand before committing to large quantities.

**Overlooking lining:** Sheer and heavily embroidered fabrics may require additional lining for comfort and coverage.

## Building a Digital Fabric Lookbook

A well-organized lookbook can help designers compare materials and plan future collections.

Each entry should include:

- Fabric name and category.
- Close-up photographs.
- Fibre composition, where known.
- Available colours.
- Width and weight.
- Suitable garment types.
- Construction and care notes.
- Supplier details.

Organizing this information makes fabric selection more efficient and reduces repeated sourcing research.

# Sourcing Notes: Building an Indian Wedding Fabric Collection

Choosing fabric for a wedding outfit is often treated as a visual decision: find a colour, choose an embroidery style and then select a textile.

In practice, fabric sourcing involves many more considerations.

The same colour can behave differently across organza, georgette, net, brocade or silk. Texture, weight, transparency, drape and surface finish can all change the final appearance of an outfit.

These notes document some of the factors to consider when exploring fabrics for Indian wedding and occasion wear.

## Start With the End Use

The first sourcing question should be:

**What is the fabric going to become?**

A fabric intended for a heavily embroidered bridal lehenga has different requirements from one intended for a lightweight dupatta.

- Lehengas: structure, weight and embroidery compatibility
- Dupattas: drape, transparency and border compatibility
- Blouses: stability, surface texture and construction
- Sarees: fall, movement and comfort
- Jackets: structure and ability to hold the intended silhouette
- Overlays: lightness, transparency and layering

Starting with the final garment makes fabric selection more practical.

## Organza Sourcing Notes

Organza is interesting because its crisp structure can create volume without necessarily making an outfit visually heavy.

When sourcing organza, consider:

- Crispness
- Transparency
- Surface finish
- Colour consistency
- Embroidery compatibility
- How the fabric behaves when gathered or layered

It can be particularly effective for contemporary bridal dupattas, sleeves, overlays and structured silhouettes.

## Georgette Sourcing Notes

Georgette is primarily about movement.

When evaluating it, drape becomes more important than simply looking at the colour on a swatch.

Useful things to examine include:

- Flow
- Weight
- Surface texture
- Opacity
- Movement
- Compatibility with embellishment

Its softer movement makes it useful for flowing lehengas, sarees, dupattas and evening outfits.

## Net Sourcing Notes

Net is often selected when a design needs transparency or layering.

When sourcing net, examine:

**Mesh structure → softness → transparency → strength → embroidery compatibility**

A softer net can create a very different silhouette from a more structured variety.

For embellished wedding garments, the ability of the fabric to support embroidery and additional surface work is particularly important.

## Brocade Sourcing Notes

Brocade is different from many lightweight wedding fabrics because the woven pattern itself can become a major design feature.

When sourcing brocade, look at:

- Pattern scale
- Repeat size
- Metallic detailing
- Fabric weight
- Reverse side
- Drape
- Colour combination

Brocade can work particularly well when the objective is to create a traditional appearance without relying entirely on additional embellishment.

## Chanderi and Silk

Chanderi and silk offer another direction for wedding textiles.

Chanderi can provide a lighter, refined character, making it useful for sarees, dupattas and festive dressing.

Silk offers a broad range of possibilities depending on the variety, weave and finish. It can be used for traditional bridal clothing as well as contemporary occasion wear.

For both categories, the actual hand-feel and drape are important considerations that photographs cannot always communicate accurately.

## What to Look For When Sourcing Fabric

### Texture

How does the fabric feel when handled?

### Drape

Does it fall naturally or hold its shape?

### Weight

Will it work with the intended garment?

### Transparency

Is the level of sheerness appropriate?

### Surface

Is it matte, glossy, textured, embroidered or woven?

### Embellishment

Can it support zari, sequins, beads or embroidery?

### Colour

Does the colour remain consistent under different lighting?

### Practicality

Will the final garment remain comfortable after several hours of wear?

## Why Fabric Swatches Matter

Digital photographs are useful for discovering fabrics, but they cannot completely communicate texture and movement.

A fabric swatch allows you to examine the textile more closely.

Ideally, check a swatch under:

- Natural daylight
- Indoor lighting
- Warm lighting
- Different viewing angles

This is particularly relevant for fabrics with sheen, metallic elements or transparent construction.

## From Fabric to Finished Outfit

One of the most interesting parts of textile sourcing is seeing how a fabric changes once it becomes part of a garment.

A small swatch may not immediately communicate the final effect.

Once cut, layered, gathered, embroidered or draped, the same textile can develop a completely different visual character.

That is why fabric selection should happen alongside the design process rather than as an afterthought.

## Final Sourcing Notes

There is no single fabric that works for every wedding outfit.

The right choice depends on the **occasion, silhouette, embroidery, season, movement and desired visual effect**.

For Indian wedding wear, fabrics such as **organza, georgette, net, brocade, Chanderi and silk** each offer different possibilities.

The most useful sourcing habit is simple:

**Don't just ask whether a fabric looks beautiful. Ask what the fabric can do.**
